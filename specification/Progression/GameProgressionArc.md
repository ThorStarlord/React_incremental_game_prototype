# Game Progression Arc — Campaign One / 1.0

**Status:** CURRENT AUTHORITY — PROVISIONAL / HUMAN-UNVALIDATED  
**Parent:** `../GameCompletionDefinition.md`  
**Feature scope:** `../Features/FeatureScopeMatrix.md`  
**Prepared:** 2026-09-11

## Purpose

The repository already has strong local progression mechanics. This document defines the **whole-game transformation** those mechanics must produce from New Game to the Campaign One finale.

The progression target is not “accumulate every system.” It is:

```text
personal action
-> relationship-shaped specialization
-> networked mastery
-> strategic synthesis
```

## Governing progression invariant

> **Automation compresses understood repetition; it does not replace discovery, interpretation, consequential choice, or irreversible agency.**

Relationship history explains learning provenance. Trait state owns durable capability. Gameplay determines local applicability. Routine familiarity records personal mastery. Copy eligibility owns execution readiness. The player chooses consequential actions and delegation.

## Stage 1 — Personal Agency

### Player state

The protagonist begins constrained and must act personally.

Expected experience:

- meet important local characters;
- travel and solve concrete problems directly;
- make the first consequential dialogue/quest choices;
- establish early Relationship Experiences;
- see at least one Experience mature into durable remembered evidence;
- encounter capability-learning conditions before having a broad build;
- perform useful routines personally;
- use baseline combat/quest solutions without requiring special Traits.

### Systems emphasized

```text
Dialogue
Quest
Travel
Relationship Experience
Memory
basic Essence
baseline Combat
```

### Systems intentionally de-emphasized

```text
large Copy management
broad Trait catalog
complex institutional/world consequence dashboards
late-game delegation
```

### Stage-1 exit

The player has personally experienced the core causal grammar:

```text
I did something meaningful
-> this relationship remembers it
-> that history changes what becomes possible
```

The transition should occur through ordinary play, not a tutorial pop-up declaring the architecture.

## Stage 2 — Relationship-Shaped Specialization

### Player state

The player now has multiple meaningful relationships and begins expressing a build shaped by **who taught them and what happened**.

Expected experience:

- multiple anchor relationships carry distinct histories;
- first permanent relationship-derived capabilities become available;
- at least one capability changes a quest/investigation solution;
- at least one capability changes tactical or other active gameplay;
- old Memories return as causal prerequisites/callbacks;
- per-NPC Knowledge begins to diverge;
- at least one institutional consequence becomes independent from personal approval;
- first routine familiarity is earned through direct practice;
- first deliberate Copy delegation becomes possible.

### Systems emphasized

```text
Relationship Memory
Trait assimilation / Resonance
Player Insight / provenance
Knowledge
Faction Reputation
Copy familiarity / first delegation
```

### Buildcraft requirement

The player must begin making a real choice between capability identities rather than simply collecting a linear mandatory list.

A capability qualifies as meaningful buildcraft when it changes at least one of:

- legal action/solution availability;
- tactical action set;
- information/interpretation opportunity;
- relationship or institutional approach;
- resource/delegation strategy.

Pure numeric bonuses may support a capability but do not satisfy the buildcraft floor alone.

### Stage-2 exit

The player can plausibly answer:

> “My character can do this because of what happened with these people.”

Human comprehension of that sentence remains an empirical Beta question; the game state and presentation must at least make the causal relationship expressible.

## Stage 3 — Networked Mastery

### Player state

The protagonist has become a networked operator rather than a lone problem-solver.

Expected experience:

- several relationships matter simultaneously;
- old decisions produce long-horizon callbacks;
- different NPCs know different objective facts;
- Faction standing and personal relationships may point in different directions;
- objective World State changes independently from either;
- capabilities combine to create more than one viable strategic approach;
- multiple routine identities have been personally mastered;
- Copies handle selected repetition;
- offline continuation advances only already-authorized safe work;
- the player spends proportionally more attention on novelty, strategy, and relationships than on solved repetition.

### Systems emphasized

```text
multi-NPC Relationship history
Knowledge divergence / transfer
Faction consequences
World State
capability combinations
multiple mastered routines
Copy priority / delegation
bounded offline continuation
```

### Copy-network target

The network does not need a generalized AI planner.

For 1.0, networked mastery means the player can maintain more than one useful Copy/routine relationship and make explicit choices about what known work deserves delegation. Each Copy remains bounded by existing eligibility and task authority.

### Stage-3 exit

The player has enough network leverage and accumulated consequences that a planetary-scale problem can credibly consume their prior history.

## Stage 4 — Strategic Synthesis / Convergence

### Player state

The campaign stops introducing unrelated progression pillars. Existing authorities converge toward the Telluric Echo crisis.

Expected experience:

- earlier Relationship Memories become preparation assets or constraints;
- capability choices influence legal preparation/encounter strategies;
- information asymmetry matters in deciding who can coordinate what;
- institutional standing affects available support/friction;
- World State determines objective conditions;
- mastered routines can be delegated to reduce preparation grind;
- meaningful final decisions remain manual;
- the finale consumes prior state rather than replacing it with a universal endgame meter.

### Systems emphasized

Everything already earned, composed selectively.

No new major progression system should debut here unless the campaign cannot resolve without it and the completion authority is revised.

## Finale progression contract

The Telluric Echo finale should test **strategic identity**, not inventory completeness.

A legal finale path should be influenced by a subset of:

```text
Relationship history
permanent capability build
who knows critical facts
Faction support/opposition
objective regional conditions
player location / active preparation
mastered delegated support work
```

The climax may converge on the broad Campaign One sacrifice/alliance beat from `Narrative/Synopsis.md`, but the preparation, available tactics, costs, and epilogue consequences must preserve meaningful prior state.

## Post-finale state

1.0 does not require a playable post-sacrifice rebuilding campaign.

Required:

- clear campaign-complete state;
- persisted completion;
- state-responsive epilogue;
- enough consequence display that the player can see how their network/history shaped the ending.

Deferred:

- interplanetary Alliance campaign;
- rebuilding authentic connections after the sacrifice;
- emergent-AI campaign;
- Dragon God relay progression;
- New Game+.

## 1.0 progression breadth floors

### Relationship network

All six anchor NPCs must participate in at least one long-horizon consequence or callback:

```text
Elder Willow
Lyra
Elara
Gronk
Silas
Valerius
```

### Capability buildcraft

Minimum:

- 4 durable relationship-derived capability identities;
- at least 3 source relationships represented;
- at least 2 capabilities with meaningful cross-domain use;
- at least 2 viable capability/build profiles by late campaign.

A “build profile” is a materially different set of useful capabilities/approaches, not a cosmetic loadout name.

### Earned delegation

Minimum:

- 3 learnable routine identities;
- at least 2 source contexts for learning;
- at least one point where choosing what to delegate creates meaningful attention/resource prioritization;
- no autonomous irreversible choices.

## Resource/economy doctrine

Campaign One does not need a generalized economy simulation.

Gold, Essence, Trait costs, quest rewards, and routine rewards exist to support the progression arc. Tuning should answer:

- does the next meaningful decision arrive at a healthy pace?
- does the player understand what resource pressure represents?
- does delegation relieve solved repetition without trivializing the active game?

Do not add currencies merely to create more progression layers.

## Combat doctrine

Combat remains a bounded active RPG tool inside the broader narrative/incremental game.

1.0 requires:

- baseline viable actions;
- relationship-derived capabilities that can alter tactical solution space where authored;
- enough encounter variety to support campaign tension;
- no requirement for a generalized equipment/crafting endgame.

## Progression anti-patterns

Do not solve progression gaps by default with:

- another universal XP bar;
- another currency;
- a parallel generic skill tree;
- large item tiers;
- automatic connection-level increases from affinity accumulation;
- time-only routine mastery;
- offline story resolution;
- mandatory collection of every Trait;
- endgame requirements unrelated to prior relationship history.

## Progression completion test

The complete Campaign One progression arc is successful at the implementation level when a fresh save can legally demonstrate:

```text
personal action
-> meaningful Relationship evidence
-> durable capability acquisition
-> capability-altered choice
-> personally mastered routine
-> explicit delegation
-> long-horizon multi-domain consequence
-> strategic finale preparation
-> state-responsive campaign ending
```

Whether that arc is understandable, satisfying, well paced, or fun remains human evidence under issue #109 and the Beta contract.
