## Purpose

This document turns the broad Campaign One narrative into a bounded **beginning-to-ending content spine** that can be implemented without converting every chapter into the same engine shape.

The campaign is authored. Heterogeneous chapter composition is a feature, not a defect.

## Campaign promise

Campaign One dramatizes the protagonist's transition from treating connection primarily as leverage toward discovering that relationship history produces forms of capability, coordination, and strategic resilience that cannot be reduced to a single approval score.

The Telluric Echo is the campaign-level pressure that forces the player's relationship network, learned capabilities, information paths, institutional consequences, world consequences, and delegation choices to converge.

## Scope

The 1.0 spine is capped at:

```text
Prologue
Chapter 1 — Merchant District Crisis       [integrated]
Chapter 2 — Archive Inquiry                [integrated]
Chapter 3 — Enemies in Phase               [integrated]
Chapter 4 — Lattice Under Strain           [new]
Chapter 5 — The Chrono-Crypt               [new]
Chapter 6 — Network Under Pressure         [new]
Chapter 7 — Counterphase                   [new]
Finale — The Telluric Echo                 [new]
Epilogue — Aftermath / Conditional Reprieve [new]
```

Names for new units are working production names and may change without changing scope or function.

## Structural rule

Not every chapter must contain every system. A chapter must justify its systems through its dramatic problem. The campaign as a whole must satisfy the completion definition.

Do not introduce a generic `ChapterEngine`, universal chapter reducer, narrative DSL, or chapter-local shadow progression merely to make these units structurally identical.

## Prologue — The Fall / First Anchor

**Function:** establish the protagonist as a constrained active agent on the isolated planet and teach the first causal relationship loop through normal play.

Required beats:

- New Game enters ordinary playable UI without debug setup;
- the player receives a clear immediate problem;
- Elder Willow or another established local anchor provides the first meaningful relationship context;
- the player performs at least one active action through dialogue, travel, quest work, or combat as appropriate;
- a consequential action produces visible Relationship evidence;
- the player sees that history persists rather than disappearing after dialogue;
- the wider Telluric Echo problem may be foreshadowed without requiring repository terminology.

**Progression role:** Stage 1 — Personal Agency.

**Exit:** a fresh player can reach Chapter 1 through ordinary UI and has encountered the causal grammar `action -> remembered consequence`.

## Chapter 1 — Merchant District Crisis

**Status:** already integrated as the first complete bounded chapter vertical slice.

Campaign role:

- proves the player can alter both personal and institutional conditions;
- introduces the difference between Relationship, Knowledge, Faction and World State;
- establishes personally learned routine -> Copy delegation;
- gives later chapters concrete local history to consume.

Future integration work: connect its legal entry and exit to the Campaign One spine without rewriting its qualified authorities.

## Chapter 2 — Archive Inquiry

**Status:** already integrated as a heterogeneous two-route chapter projection centered on Elara.

Campaign role:

- makes interpretation/evidence a relationship problem;
- deepens causal legibility and epistemic capability buildcraft;
- supports `ScholarlyInsight` provenance;
- provides evidence that can later identify or reinterpret Telluric Echo behavior;
- contrasts distinct histories of model challenge, consensus and revision.

**Progression role:** Stage 2 — Relationship-Shaped Specialization.

Future integration work: give Archive Inquiry a natural campaign trigger from Chapter 1 consequences and a legal exit into the escalating Echo/Lyra thread.

## Chapter 3 — Enemies in Phase

**Status:** already integrated as a structurally different Lyra-centered one-route projection.

Campaign role:

- turns adversarial relationship history into necessary calibration;
- moves Lyra from pure rival toward a strategically indispensable counterpart;
- demonstrates that conflict can create deep relational evidence without collapsing into simple positive affinity;
- provides the bridge to cooperative Chrono-Crypt play.

**Progression role:** late Stage 2 / transition to Stage 3.

Future integration work: connect its qualifying Lyra history to campaign-visible Echo escalation and the reason the player cannot solve the next problem alone.

## Chapter 4 — Lattice Under Strain

**Function:** make the Telluric Echo a present campaign problem by causing selective pressure on the network the player has already built.

Required system roles:

- consume prior Relationship history from multiple anchors;
- consume at least one Archive/Knowledge result;
- expose different NPC awareness of the Echo problem;
- produce at least one institutional reaction independent from personal Relationship;
- change at least one objective regional World State condition;
- make at least two legal response strategies possible through existing capabilities or baseline action;
- create no generalized “Echo meter” unless repeated implementation evidence proves a persistent objective value is required.

**Anchor emphasis:** Willow, Elara, Gronk/Silas/Valerius as appropriate; Lyra remains relevant without needing to dominate the chapter.

**Progression role:** Stage 3 — Networked Mastery.

**Exit:** the player learns that ordinary local responses cannot fully address the Echo and obtains a legal reason to enter the Chrono-Crypt with Lyra.

## Chapter 5 — The Chrono-Crypt

**Function:** concentrate the Lyra relationship and capability-build problem in a bounded authored location.

Required system roles:

- authored travel/access into the crypt;
- adversarial/cooperative Lyra Relationship callbacks;
- at least one capability-sensitive puzzle, investigation, or combat problem;
- baseline viability without one mandatory golden Trait;
- meaningful opportunity for capability combination or different build approaches;
- no generalized time-dilation/offline narrative simulation requirement;
- at least one durable Relationship/Memory consequence that matters in the finale;
- reveal the counterphase principle needed to confront the Echo.

**Progression role:** Stage 3 — Networked Mastery.

**Exit:** the player and Lyra emerge with a credible countermeasure theory but insufficient network preparation to execute it.

## Chapter 6 — Network Under Pressure

**Function:** make the player's whole local network matter before the finale.

Required system roles:

- consume at least three anchor-NPC histories;
- use Knowledge asymmetry as a real decision constraint;
- make institutional standing relevant independently from personal relationships;
- consume/change objective World State;
- surface capability/build differences in non-identical ways;
- require personally mastered routine work in preparation;
- allow selected preparation repetition to be delegated to Copies;
- keep novel political/social/strategic decisions manual.

**Progression role:** late Stage 3.

**Exit:** the player has chosen which parts of the network to stabilize, inform, mobilize, or leave exposed. These choices shape Chapter 7 and the epilogue.

## Chapter 7 — Counterphase

**Function:** convert accumulated history into explicit finale preparation without adding a new progression pillar.

Required system roles:

- read prior canonical authorities rather than synthesize one universal readiness currency;
- expose multiple legal preparation routes;
- make capability/build choices materially affect available preparation actions;
- let Copies handle only mastered safe preparation routines;
- preserve the player's authority over final commitments;
- explain why major preparation options are available without exposing hidden future outcomes;
- establish the cost/risk of the final confrontation.

**Progression role:** Stage 4 — Strategic Synthesis.

**Exit:** a legal player-owned commitment starts the finale.

## Finale — The Telluric Echo

**Function:** pay off the Campaign One systems and narrative promise.

The finale must:

- be reachable from a fresh save through legal normal-UI progression;
- consume prior Relationship history;
- consume permanent capability/build state;
- use Knowledge/Faction/World State where relevant to preparation or available support;
- preserve at least two viable strategic preparation/tactical profiles;
- never require every optional capability;
- preserve meaningful player agency at the irreversible commitment point;
- never auto-resolve through offline progression or Copy autonomy;
- make the broad counterphase/network-inversion resolution from the existing synopsis compatible with the actual player's history rather than a fixed sequence that ignores it.

### Convergence policy

1.0 does not require several completely separate finales.

It does require that different campaign histories alter some combination of:

- who can help;
- what the player understands;
- what tactics are legal;
- what costs are paid;
- what institutional/world conditions remain;
- which relationship consequences are foregrounded;
- the epilogue.

## Epilogue — Aftermath / Conditional Reprieve

**Function:** show that the game remembers how the player arrived at the ending.

At minimum, the epilogue should be capable of reflecting:

- Lyra relationship outcome/interpretation;
- at least two other anchor relationship callbacks;
- important Faction standing;
- at least one objective world consequence;
- the player's major capability/build identity or contribution;
- the state of the Copy/delegation network where narratively relevant;
- the Campaign One costly network-inversion and Alliance aftermath.

A single static paragraph independent of state does not satisfy the 1.0 completion definition.

The epilogue may seed Alliance integration, post-campaign rebuilding, emergent AI conflict, Dragon God relay infrastructure, or interplanetary travel. These seeds must not require playable implementation in 1.0.

## Campaign-wide content requirements

### Anchor cast

Every anchor NPC requires:

1. a meaningful introduction or existing production presence;
2. at least one consequential player interaction;
3. persistent Relationship evidence where relationally meaningful;
4. at least one later callback/consumer before or during the finale/epilogue.

Not every NPC needs equal screen time or a Trait.

### Buildcraft

Campaign content must support four durable relationship-derived capabilities across at least three relationships, with at least two cross-domain capabilities and at least two viable late-game build profiles.

### Delegation

Campaign content must support at least three mastered routines across at least two contexts. Delegation increasingly removes solved repetition while final decisions remain player-owned.

### Causal legibility

When an important option is visible because of prior player-earned state, the game should provide player-facing causal explanation when useful. Hidden future requirements remain hidden.

## New-chapter acceptance template

Every new chapter package must preregister:

```text
Dramatic problem
Entry requirements
Primary anchor characters
Player-owned consequential choices
Canonical authorities consumed
Canonical authorities changed
Capability/build relevance
Routine/delegation relevance, if any
Long-horizon callbacks
Exit conditions
Negative/rejection paths
Save/load expectations
Human-evidence ceiling
```

## Campaign anti-expansion rules

- Do not add a new chapter because the architecture can support one.
- Do not add a new system because a chapter could showcase it.
- Do not force every chapter to exercise all domain authorities.
- Do not use a universal chapter-complete boolean to erase the causal state that actually establishes completion.
- Do not make Copy/offline systems resolve narrative judgment.

## Campaign completion criterion

The campaign architecture is implementation-complete when a normal fresh save can legally traverse:

```text
New Game
-> Prologue
-> Chapters 1–7
-> Telluric Echo finale
-> state-responsive Epilogue
-> persisted campaign-complete state
```

without debug injection, impossible prerequisite knowledge, or developer intervention.
