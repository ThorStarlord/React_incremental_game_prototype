# Post-UI4 scenario stress test: three divergent, non-canon game directions

**Status:** SYNTHETIC DESIGN STRESS TEST / NON-CANON CONCEPTS / NO PLAYER EXECUTION.
**Scope:** A Relationship-derived builds; B Knowledge and information strategy; C World/Faction responsiveness.
**Source:** PostUI4DivergentExploration.md; canonical domain authority in Relationship, Trait, Knowledge, Faction and World State specs; released UI4 baseline main d3b397f9.
**Not:** new story canon, content IDs, game code, an authorized 1.1 milestone, or evidence of participant preference.

## A. The lesson remembered when the plan fails

**Premise (illustrative, not canon):** A patrol route follows the same repeating error. Willow's Wisdom suggests that repairing the cycle is different from defeating one opponent. Scholarly Insight can reveal contradictory accounts of how the route was planned. A player with either earned capability can use it in a different way; a player with neither retains a viable ordinary method.

**Two meaningful choices:**

- Follow the repeating pattern and intervene at the release point, using an earned pattern-reading Trait if present. The scene visibly recalls which prior relationship experience taught it. This is not a free success button: the player must still select an intervention.
- Compare contradictory reports before acting, using an earned analysis capability if present; the player can instead choose the slower ordinary verification path. The two approaches may produce different credible accounts and social costs.

**Canon ownership:** Relationship and Memory own learning evidence; permanent Trait state owns capability; the encounter or Quest owns applicability; Knowledge owns who can verify the accounts; Chapter projection displays only already-earned information. A selective expression rule exists only on the separate, unqualified 1.1 draft, not on released 1.0.

**What must be visible:** Why the option exists, what the player has learned, what is still uncertain, which choice they made and a changed opportunity later. The story should not turn into a full causal-debugger table.

**Potential value:** Capabilities retain identities derived from people, rather than being small numeric bonuses. **Falsifying risks:** both choices merely grant the same generic success; ordinary route becomes punishment for not grinding a relationship; the text spoils hidden requirements; 1.1 expression changes invalidate old save history.

**Smallest discriminating experiment:** two authored decision tables and one simulated-player comparison of actual player-visible prerequisites/cause, without a new Trait, generic capability graph or production content implementation.

## B. The disputed archive report

**Premise (illustrative, not canon):** A document could explain a trading discrepancy, but one witness has seen only part of the evidence. Another contact can verify it; an institution wants a rapid decision.

**Two meaningful choices:**

- Disclose the incomplete report immediately to a trusted contact or institution. The recipient can act sooner but may form an incomplete conclusion; the decision is attributable to the player.
- Withhold the incomplete conclusion long enough to seek verification, at the cost of delayed action or lost opportunity. Later disclosure changes who can act and on what evidence.

**Canon ownership:** Knowledge owns per-NPC awareness of objective facts; Relationship owns trust and personal interpretation; Faction owns institutional access/standing; objective World State owns what actually changed. Dialogue/Quest choices consume these authorities without generic rumor propagation.

**What must be visible:** This person knows X; that claim remains unverified; the player can see who was told; later someone reacts to the actual evidence they received. Never turn withheld information into secret knowledge automatically available to all NPCs.

**Potential value:** Strategic choices are about information, timing and trust rather than raw currency. **Falsifying risks:** the system hides the information needed to make any informed decision; options become an obvious correct vs wrong response; knowledge and belief are accidentally conflated; a general epistemic engine is required for one scene.

**Smallest discriminating experiment:** single authored branching information dilemma and a skeptical simulated-agent causal probe, using existing per-NPC Knowledge; no global rumor system.

## C. Returning to a changed district

**Premise (illustrative, not canon):** The player revisits the Merchant District after a consequential decision. Prior public-order vs quiet-network actions already altered objective conditions and faction reactions.

**Two meaningful choice shapes:**

- Work openly through institutional permission. The district visibly offers an accessible contact/service, but a different character may interpret the arrangement skeptically.
- Rely on quieter individual cooperation. Some public services are less accessible, but a learned contact or private approach is available. Neither route should simply be the "good" or "bad" ending.

**Canon ownership:** World State owns objective trade/watch conditions; Faction owns institutional terms; Relationship owns personal responses; Knowledge owns what contacts actually know. Travel remains legal adjacent routes; no NPC schedules are inferred. Do not create a generalized inventory, goods-pricing or economy simulator.

**What must be visible:** A short changed place description, one consequential legal action difference, and a recognizable connection to past choices. A decorative background swap alone is insufficient.

**Potential value:** The city and characters acquire continuity; choices matter in places the player can imagine inhabiting. **Falsifying risks:** atmospheric prose has no operational consequences; players cannot locate the source of the change; different Faction/World/Relationship layers are collapsed into one status; authoring every district state multiplies content unmanageably.

**Smallest discriminating experiment:** an A/B return-visit vignette on paper with one actual available action per route. Ask simulated agents to infer what caused the difference without showing the upstream spec.

## Cross-concept comparison

| Dimension | A Relationship build | B Information choice | C Reactive place |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Distinctive action | apply person-taught competence | manage who knows, verifies and acts | choose how to engage a changed place |
| Existing state reused | Relationship, Memory, Trait, Quest/Combat | Knowledge, Relationship, Faction | World State, Faction, Relationship, Travel |
| Main player payoff hypothesis | "My past relationships define what I can do" | "Trust and information change strategy" | "The world remembers my decisions" |
| Most serious risk | opaque capability gate / 1.1 scope migration | unfair hidden information | ornamental consequence |
| First evidence to seek | different visible solutions with viable baseline | action distinguishes facts from beliefs | observable change in an actual affordance |
| Abstraction to avoid | generic skill/capability graph | general rumor or NPC belief engine | economic, schedule or world simulation |

**Working synthesis:** A can supply the player-owned ability, B the meaningful dilemma, and C the durable world response. D (earned Copy delegation) should compress only already-understood repetition between such decisions. This is **a hypothesis for evaluation**, not a commitment to build a three-system feature.

**Next bounded action:** use the six-profile **current** simulated-agent campaign to identify which misunderstanding or absence of consequential choices is most important. Then implement, if warranted, a single reversible authored case on a versioned 1.1 branch with explicit authority, regression and exact-head qualification. Preserve the original v1.0.0 tag and human-review claim ceiling.
